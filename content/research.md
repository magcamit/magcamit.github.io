My primary research interest is in Critical Security Studies with thematic expertise in violent conflicts, international trade, and foreign policy in the Indo-Pacific. More specifically, I focus on the often invisible albeit powerful factors/mechanisms that are vital to understanding these issues such as _affect and ethnoreligious nationalism_ (in violent conflicts), _insecurity and anxiety_ (in international trade), and _populism and securitisation_ (in foreign policy).

My research projects on ethnoreligious otherings and violent conflicts and the (in)security politics of international trade (funded by the European Commission Horizon 2020 Programme and the New Zealand Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade, respectively) led to the  publication of my two books, _Ethnoreligious Otherings and Passionate Conflicts_ (Oxford University Press 2022) and _Small Powers and Trading Security_ (Palgrave/Springer 2016 and 2018). My work probing these nontraditional dimensions of international security has also appeared in journals such as the _International Studies Quarterly_, _International Affairs_, _Political Science_, _International Politics_, _Critical Studies on Security_, and _International Relations of the Asia-Pacific_, among others.

Currently, I am working on a research project, entitled, &#39;Decolonising Peacebuilding through the Affective Peace Framework&#39;, that is being funded by the Leverhulme Trust. In collaboration with Hiroshima University (IDEC), I am exploring the potential utility and function of affect-based approaches towards decolonising traditional peacebuilding programmes and inititatives in Southeast Asia&#39;s conflict-affected communities.
